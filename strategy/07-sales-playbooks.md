# 07 — Consultative sales and partnership playbooks

_Date: 2026-10-10. Based on `01`–`06`. Tags: [V] verified · [H] hypothesis · [U] unknown._

> **Principles.** VIVIA is an **unvalidated prototype**; these playbooks are for **learning, not selling**. Never claim clinical validation, time savings, accuracy figures, certification or regulatory compliance (none exist: `04` §4). Never pressure anyone to share health information. Prices below are **test frameworks, not validated market rates**. Scripts are in English; **Italian versions must be prepared and reviewed by a native speaker before use.**

**Ground rules for every conversation**
1. Say who you are, what VIVIA is, that it is early, and what you want in the first minute.
2. Ask before recording; record only with consent; keep notes without names.
3. Ask about **experiences and habits**, not diagnoses. Do not request documents.
4. Offer a clear exit at any time. Say what will happen to the notes.
5. Do not ask for a decision on a first call. A "no" or "not for me" is useful data: record the reason.
6. Follow up once, within 48 hours. Do not chase.

## Playbook A — Patients (research interview, usability test, beta)

**Goal:** a 30-minute interview or a prototype test. **Not** a sale.

### A1. Respectful introduction (message)
> "Hi [name], I'm [founder]. I'm developing a small tool to help people with Crohn's or ulcerative colitis organise their information and prepare for medical visits. It's at a very early stage and I'm talking with people before building more. Would you be open to a 30-minute chat about how you manage this today? I won't ask for any medical records or private health details, you can stop any time, and I'll keep what you say anonymous. If it's not for you, no problem at all."

### A2. Opening (first 2 minutes)
"Thank you. Quick overview: I'll ask how you manage things now, what works and what doesn't. There are no right answers. You can skip any question. May I take notes? May I record audio for my own notes only? (If no: notes only.)"

### A3. Discovery questions (pick 8–10)

**Current IBD management**
1. How do you keep track of your care between visits?
2. What is the hardest part of managing things day to day? (Let them choose; do not name symptoms first.)

**Workarounds and existing apps**
3. Have you used any app, spreadsheet or notebook for this? What made you start, and what made you stop (if you did)?
4. If you use an app now, what does it do well, and what annoys you?

**Documents and doctor visits**
5. Tell me about the last visit. What did you bring? What did you forget to say?
6. Where do your reports and test results live (paper, photos, hospital portal, email)?
7. How do you explain your history to a new professional?

**Daily life**
8. When did you last need to find a toilet quickly? What did you do?

**Privacy and trust**
9. Would you put reports or notes into a new app? What would you need to see first?
10. Who should be able to see your information, and who should not?

**Price (only if they are comfortable)**
11. Have you paid for a health app or service? What made it worthwhile? Who do you think should pay for tools like this?

### A4. Short explanation of VIVIA (30 seconds)
"VIVIA is an early prototype. You can record how you are in a few seconds, keep your reports and treatments in one timeline where each item shows where it came from, and prepare a one-page summary with your own questions for a visit. You choose what to share and you can delete everything. It's an organising tool: it doesn't diagnose or give treatment advice. It's early, so it will have rough edges."

### A5. The testing request
"Would you be willing to try a prototype with example data, for about 20 minutes, while I watch and ask what's confusing? Using real health information isn't needed and I'm not asking for it. Later, if you want, you could be on a list for a small test group; there's no obligation."

### A6. Objection handling

| What they say | Respond | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| "I don't want to share health data." | "That's completely fair. You don't need to share any: I'll use sample data and ask for your reactions." | Push for real data |
| "I already use an app." | "Great, tell me what it's good at, I'd learn a lot. Nothing to switch." | Criticise their app |
| "I don't have time." | "I understand. Even 15 minutes helps; or we can do it later." | Pressure |
| "What are you going to do with the data?" | "In this chat, nothing is stored with your name. In the prototype, you'd control consent, export and deletion. I can show you the privacy settings." | Over-promise about security or compliance |
| "Is this a medical app?" | "No. It helps organise information and doesn't diagnose or advise on treatment." | Imply clinical benefit |
| "Is it free?" | "Pricing isn't decided. I'm learning what would be fair and who should pay. You owe nothing." | Quote a price as final |
| "Will my doctor accept it?" | "I don't know yet; that's something I'm also asking doctors." | Claim clinical endorsement |
| "I'm not feeling well / this is too much." | "Thank you, and no pressure. We can stop now or pick another day." | Continue |

### A7. Polite closing
"Thank you, this helped. I'll send a short summary of what I learned in the coming weeks. If you'd like to see the prototype or join the test group, tell me; if not, I'll keep your details only as long as you agree, and delete them on request."

### A8. Follow-up message (within 48 h)
"Thank you for your time yesterday. Your point about [specific, non-sensitive theme] will help us. As promised, I haven't kept any private details. Would you like to be told when the prototype is ready to test, or prefer I don't contact you again? Either answer is fine."

## Playbook B — Gastroenterologists and IBD nurses

**Goal:** understand their workflow and explore whether a patient-prepared summary is useful. **Do not claim** clinical validation, time savings or accuracy.

### B1. Introduction
> "Dr [name] / [name], I'm [founder], developing a prototype that helps people with IBD prepare a one-page summary and their own questions before a visit. I'm not here to sell anything and I make no clinical claims. I'd value 20 minutes of your view on what would help in a consultation, what would not, and what I'm missing."

### B2. Discovery questions

**Appointments**
1. How long is a typical IBD follow-up? What is covered, and what often gets left out?
2. What information do you most need at the start of a visit and often don't have?

**Patient-reported information**
3. How do patients tell you about symptoms between visits today? Do you use questionnaires or scores? Do you see diaries, app exports or screenshots? How are they received?
4. Do patients raise fatigue, urgency, pain or mood? How are those handled in the time available?

**Documents and follow-up**
5. How do you get external reports and test results? What is missing most often?
6. After a visit, how do you follow up between appointments (nurse line, email, portal)?

**Workflow, integration, security, liability**
7. If a patient arrived with a one-page summary, where would you want to see it (printed, link, in the record)? What must it never contain?
8. What would an IT/data-protection review require before you or your department used such a tool?
9. What are your concerns about liability when patient-entered data or AI-assisted organisation is used?
10. What would you need to see to consider recommending it to a patient (evidence, safeguards, references)?

### B3. Description of VIVIA (clear, with limits)
"A patient-facing web app, early prototype. Patients log quickly, keep treatments and reports in one timeline with the source of each item, and generate a summary with their questions. Each value taken from a document is confirmed by the patient; the app does not interpret results. AI is optional and off by default and is currently tested only with synthetic data. No clinician portal yet. No clinical evaluation has been done."

### B4. The request
"Could I show you the prototype with example data for 15 minutes and ask what's wrong with it? If useful, we could explore a small, designed-by-you pilot later; nothing now."

### B5. Objection handling

| Concern | Response | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| "I have no time." | "That's exactly what I'm trying to understand; I'd value even ten minutes, or a note from your nurse." | Claim to save time |
| "Is it accurate?" | "Each extracted value is confirmed by the patient and labelled with its source. I have not yet measured accuracy; that's one of the things I'd like your help testing." | Quote accuracy numbers |
| "Does it integrate with our system?" | "Not now. It exports a PDF or an expiring link. Integration would depend on your IT; I'd like to learn the requirements." | Promise integration |
| "What about privacy and GDPR?" | "Patients give consent per purpose, can export and delete. It hasn't had an independent data-protection or security review yet; that's a prerequisite for any real-data pilot." | Claim compliance or certification |
| "Who is liable?" | "I don't know yet. That's a legal question I need to settle before any pilot; I'd like your institution's view." | Wave away liability |
| "Patients will bring junk." | "That's a risk. The summary is kept short and sourced; I'd like to hear what would make it worthless." | Argue |
| "Is it a medical device?" | "It's designed as an organising tool with no diagnosis or treatment advice. Legal and regulatory advice is still to be obtained." | State a regulatory conclusion |

### B6. Follow-up (within 48 h)
"Thank you, [name]. I noted three things you said: [theme 1], [theme 2], [theme 3]. I'll change [X] first. If you're open to it, may I show you the next version in a month? No obligation either way."

## Playbook C — Clinics and hospitals

**Goal:** judge whether a B2B offer could solve a **meaningful operational problem**. No selling before a named problem and a sponsor.

### C1. Discovery questions
1. What are the main operational pressures in IBD care now (capacity, waiting times, follow-up, documents)?
2. Is there a current initiative to improve patient information or self-management? Who sponsors it?
3. How are patient-reported data currently collected, if at all?
4. What patient-facing digital tools does the department already offer or recommend, and who approved them?
5. What would success look like in one year, and how would you measure it?

### C2. Stakeholder map (fill in per site)

| Role | Why they matter | Typical concern | Questions to ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical lead (IBD) | Sponsor / champion | Workload, safety | Would you sponsor a 3-month pilot? |
| IBD nurse / coordinator | Day-to-day user | Time, patient questions | How would it fit your routine? |
| Hospital management | Budget, priorities | Cost, evidence | Which budget line would fund this? |
| IT / information security | Approval | Hosting, access, logs | What review is mandatory? |
| Data-protection officer | Legal basis | DPIA, processor contract | Which documents do you need from us? |
| Procurement | Process | Thresholds, tenders | What route applies at our price? (Italian rules **not researched: verify**) |
| Legal / risk | Liability, device status | Claims | What review of claims and liability? |
| Patient representative | Legitimacy | Independence | Can a patient group co-design? |

### C3. Procurement and IT/security questions
- What hosting location and certifications are required? (VIVIA has none claimed; EU hosting planned.)
- Which security assessments are mandatory (penetration test, questionnaires)?
- What data processing agreement and DPIA are required? Who completes them?
- Is single sign-on or integration with the electronic record required? What systems?
- What are the thresholds below which a pilot can be bought without a tender? **[U]**

### C4. Integration requirements to explore
Export format needed (PDF, FHIR later), authentication, audit logs, data retention, who can see patient data, and clinician-facing view. **None of these exists today** (no clinician portal; `docs/ROADMAP.md`).

### C5. Pilot design (proposal)
- **Purpose:** test whether a patient-prepared summary is useful in follow-up visits.
- **Population:** 20–40 adults with IBD recruited by the clinic, with informed consent.
- **Duration:** 3 months.
- **Measures (agreed with the clinic, not assumed):** share of patients who use it before a visit; clinician rating of usefulness per visit; patient-reported preparedness; incidents; data-protection issues. **No time-saving claim unless measured.**
- **Safeguards:** DPIA completed before data; EU hosting; no AI processing of patient data unless consent and a processor agreement exist; clinician reviews wording; a stop rule.
- **Roles:** clinic sponsor, nurse lead, VIVIA lead, data-protection officer.
- **Exit:** the clinic can stop at any time; data deleted at the end.

### C6. Budget and purchasing discovery
- Who holds the budget for patient-facing digital tools? Is there an innovation or research fund?
- Typical price range for comparable tools in your experience? (Reference only: a UK single-supplier award for a competing app was £30,554 for one year [V]; do not quote as a market rate.)
- Is there a pilot-then-contract model, or a tender?

### C7. Next-step proposal
"I'd like to propose a one-page pilot outline for your review, with the safeguards above, and a joint call with your data-protection officer to confirm what would be required. If the requirements are unrealistic for a team of our size, I'd rather know now."

## Playbook D — Patient organisations and partners

**Goal:** educational partnerships, co-design and **ethical** recruitment, with safeguards.

### D1. Transparent introduction
> "I'm [founder]. VIVIA is an early prototype to help people with Crohn's or ulcerative colitis organise information and prepare visits. It's **not** certified, **not** clinically validated and **not** a medical device. I'm talking to organisations like yours first, because I'd like patients' needs to shape it, and because I want to be sure it's something members would trust."

### D2. What is experimental / what participation involves
| Item | Statement |
|---|---|
| Status | Prototype, may contain errors |
| Data | No real health data in early tests; example data only until legal and security reviews are done |
| Participation | Optional interviews (30 min) and prototype testing (20 min) |
| Independence | VIVIA is not funded by drug companies. **Say this only while it is true**; disclose any funding change |
| Your control | Your organisation can review wording and withdraw at any time |
| What we ask | Introductions to interested members; a review of materials; optionally co-design |
| What we don't ask | Member lists, personal data, endorsement of clinical claims |

### D3. Discovery questions
1. What do your members ask for most?
2. Which digital tools do you recommend or avoid, and why?
3. What does your organisation need to see before recommending a tool (independence, privacy, evidence)?
4. Have you collaborated with companies before? What worked and what was uncomfortable?
5. How would you want members' voices represented in our design?

### D4. Objections
| Concern | Response |
|---|---|
| "Commercial interest?" | "Funding and model are undecided. I will tell you before any change. You can review and withdraw." |
| "Data protection?" | "No member data is collected through you. Participants consent individually. No real data in early tests." |
| "Evidence?" | "None yet. That is why I'm asking for co-design, not endorsement." |
| "We can't endorse." | "Understood. Even a conversation or feedback on wording helps." |

### D5. Next steps
Offer: a one-page summary, a co-design workshop with 6–8 members, and an agreement on communications (what is said about the partnership).

## 5. Pricing discussion framework (discovery, not offering)

**Principle:** do not quote prices as validated. Ask what people would pay and who should pay, then test with **pre-orders only after legal review of pre-sales** (`05` #11).

| Model | What to ask | Hypothesis range to *test* [A] | What would be evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free patient access** | "If it were free, who should fund it?" | €0 | Funder willing to cover free access (association, hospital) |
| **Premium patient subscription** | "What would make you pay? What would be unfair?" Show 3 price points (e.g. €3.99 / €5.99 / €8.99 per month, as a test, not a list price) | €0–€60 / year | ≥30% of reservers confirm a **paid** pre-order at one price |
| **Clinician subscription** | "What problem would justify a budget? Who approves?" | €0 until a pilot proves value | A clinician names a budget and signs a pilot |
| **Institutional pilot** | "What would a pilot cost you in time and money? Who funds?" | €5,000–€20,000 as a *reference to test* [A] | A signed pilot agreement |

**Rules:** state "pricing is not decided"; record exact quotes and who said them; separate "would pay" from "has paid"; do not treat polite interest as willingness to pay; do not discount to win a commitment.

## 6. Pilot proposal outline (one page, for clinics or associations)

1. **Purpose and hypothesis** — whether a patient-prepared summary is useful in follow-up visits (not a clinical-effect claim).
2. **What VIVIA is and is not** — prototype, organising tool, no diagnosis or treatment advice, no clinician portal, no regulatory status.
3. **Participants** — 20–40 adults, informed consent, voluntary, withdrawal at any time.
4. **Duration and schedule** — 3 months; check-ins at weeks 2, 6, 12.
5. **What data** — what is collected, hosting location, retention, deletion on request, no data sale, AI off unless consented.
6. **Measures agreed with the partner** — usage before visits; clinician usefulness rating; patient-reported preparedness; incidents; data-protection findings.
7. **Roles** — sponsor, nurse lead, data-protection officer, VIVIA lead.
8. **Safeguards and stop rule** — DPIA before data; clinician review of wording; stop if a safety or data-protection issue arises.
9. **Costs and funding** — to be agreed; none required for patients.
10. **Outputs** — a short report shared with the partner; the partner may publish its own view.
